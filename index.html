      Rules
      • You have 25 rounds and start with 500 gold, enough for one building, plus one perk you pick from three. Whoever has the most gold at the end wins. Goods you haven't sold count for nothing.
      • Mine sites: each round 3 sites are offered. Hillside mines are average, silver veins produce almost only silver, gem pockets give lots of gems but run dry, rich lodes are strong but also run dry, and deep shafts are expensive but produce a lot. Each mine you already own makes the next one 12% more expensive.
      • Workers: a building produces nothing without workers. Each building takes up to 2, and its output grows with their combined skill: a worker with 100% skill produces the amounts below, one with 130% produces 30% more. You are a worker too, with 100% skill and no wage. New applicants arrive every round. A worker's wage is roughly 40 × skill, give or take 30%, so some are bargains. Hired workers are paid every round, even while idle, and you can let them go at any time.
      • Experience: each round a worker spends on a task (mining, cutting, crafting, minting, glassmaking) adds +5% to their skill at that task, up to +50% after 10 rounds. Their wage stays the same. Experience is kept per task, so moving a worker elsewhere starts them fresh there, and moving them back picks up where they left off.
      • Buying raw goods: you can buy uncut gems and silver on the market for 25% above their selling price. Each unit you buy raises the price.
      • Jeweller: cuts 2 uncut gems a round. Each cut fails 10% of the time and the gem is lost. Otherwise you get a sapphire (80%) or a diamond (20%).
      • Goldsmith: crafts 2 pieces a round, either a ring (1 silver + 1 sapphire) or a necklace (2 silver + 1 diamond). On auto, it makes whichever is worth more than its materials at current prices, and nothing if neither is.
      • Mint: strikes 3 silver a round into 3 coins. Glassworks: melts 3 uncut gems a round into 3 panes of stained glass. Both sit idle while their product is worth no more than its raw material. Coins and glass lose very little price as you sell, so they suit selling in bulk.
      • Guilds: jewellers, goldsmiths, mints and glassworks each need a guild licence. You join a guild when you build your first building of that kind. You can only ever join 2 guilds, and a licence stays used even if you sell the buildings later. While you are in just one guild, its buildings produce 25% more.
      • An upgrade costs 300, up to 3 per building, and adds +20% output. On a mine it also adds +10 points to the gem chance. On a jeweller it adds +10 points to the diamond chance and lowers the failure rate by 3 points.
      • Market: each unit you sell lowers that good's price, and the price recovers 35% of the way back each round. Prices also drift at random, with occasional rushes and crashes.
      • Auto-sell: set a minimum price for a good. After the market moves each round, and as soon as you set the order, units are sold one at a time while the price is at or above your limit.
      • Events: some rounds bring an event that you must decide on before ending the round.
      • Upkeep: each mine costs 15–30 a round depending on its site type, a glassworks 15, a jeweller 20, a mint 25 and a goldsmith 30. Each kind of building you own after the first adds 10% overhead on top, and every mine site type (hillside, silver vein, …) counts as its own kind. More buildings of a kind you already own add no overhead. Wages are paid on top. If you can't pay, your most valuable goods are sold automatically. You can sell a building for 50% of what you paid for it.
      • Warehouse: holds 40 goods to start. Anything over the limit at the end of a round is lost, cheapest goods first.
      • Order at the end of each round: mines, then jewellers, glassworks, goldsmiths and mints (best-upgraded first within each kind), then workers gain experience, then the warehouse limit, then upkeep, wages and debts, then the market moves, then auto-sell orders run.
